# PART ONE: THE STORY (for builders)

## The story in five sentences

In 1889 two young sailors on the white cruiser USS Chicago, a Scots-Irish deckhand who could not swim and an Italian
electric-light hand from San Francisco who could, became inseparable, and loved each other in a way neither could say.
The deckhand went home to Monterey, married a schoolteacher he truly loved and raised a daughter; his buddy stayed in
the navy, became a diver and an electrician, stood best man at the wedding and godfather at the christening, and spent
thirty years as the family's dearest friend. When the captain's wife died in 1925 the two old men finally came
together, and the diver built a machine so the man who could not swim could come below with him as a fish; they dove
the bay's wrecks for years, and when the diver's lungs gave out they laid their life across the sea floor in
photographs and five chests, a treasure hunt that would tell the captain's grandsons, without a word, who the two of
them were. The older grandson walks the floor in the diver's old suit and the younger swims as a fish in the old
helmet, while their grandfather sails and works the pump and watches them learn his life one photograph at a time,
from two young sailors to two old men. The last chest, deepest of all, holds the picture of the two old men's own
quiet wedding, and the game ends with the boat coming into Monterey at dusk, where the diver is waiting on the wharf
to be taken aboard, and a photograph of all four of them.

## The people

All names are working names (the game shows almost none).

**The captain: Malcolm "Mac" Doyle, 67 in the game's present (1934).** Irish-Scottish: a Scottish mother (her tartan
is the scarf he wears with the cream knit), an Irish father, a Monterey boyhood. The user's: "gay captain", "bushy
chest hair and tattoos", "staunch proportions", the boys' "gay grandpa", and "in the navy". Born 1867; enlisted 1885 at
18; on the new protected cruiser USS Chicago from 1889, in the Squadron of Evolution (the "White Squadron": white hulls,
buff upperworks) on its cruise to Europe and the Mediterranean in 1889 and 1890; discharged 1892 at 25. He went home,
fished, bought a boat, married. He cannot swim (many sailors of his day could not): the joke of photograph 2 and the
reason for the machine. Stocky, slow, kind, funny without trying; everything he feels goes out through his hands and
his work.

**His buddy: Vincenzo "Vince" Rinaldi, 66.** Italian: a Genoese fishing family of San Francisco's North Beach, a
mother whose kitchen and cioppino are famous in the family, a saint's medal (Saint Christopher, the traveller's) he
has never taken off and once gave away (photograph 3), a mandolin. Born 1868; navy 1886 to 1912: the Chicago's
electric-light hand when the two met (the ship was among the first in the navy lit by electricity), later a navy
electrician and diver; at Manila Bay in 1898 on the Olympia; round the world with the Great White Fleet in 1907 to
1909 (it came into Monterey Bay in May 1908, and the Doyles rowed out to see him). After the navy he was the bay's
salvage diver, working out of Monterey. He never married. Tall and lean against Mac's barrel; a big moustache, grey
now; round spectacles in later life (both read on a mask face and give a stylised face its identity). Clever with
wires, restless, warm, the family's uncle in all but blood. He built the machine. He is alive in the recommended
version (see "What became of Vince", below).

**The captain's wife: Ellen Doyle (born Hale), 1872 to 1925.** A Monterey schoolteacher who sang in the church choir
and kept the boat's books. A real marriage and a real person: Mac loved her, they made a good life, and she loved
Vince as family (he was best man and godfather; Christmas was at his mother's table). What she knew of Mac's heart is
left unsaid, and should stay unsaid: the photographs never make her an obstacle or a fool. She died in the winter of
1925 of pneumonia. The cream cable-knit Mac wears was knitted by her; she is in four of the photographs.

**Their daughter, the boys' mother: Rose, 38.** Born 1896. Married a Carmel man in 1914. She runs the fish market at
Carmel with her husband (who can stay off screen). She knows about her father and Vince and has always been glad of
it: she was the only witness at their wedding in 1926 (photograph 9), with her two small boys. She sent her sons to
their grandfather for the summer knowing what they would find.

**The older brother: Jonah, 19.** The user: "a budding handsom curly dark haired sailor about 19", the diver, the
angler. Born 1915. He wants to be what Vince was: Vince taught him to dive the summer he was sixteen, and he wears
Vince's old hard-hat suit, refitted (the diver on the title is in it), then the better suits the shop sells. His
grandfather works his pump and hose from the deck. **He finds everything on the floor:** the photographs, the chests,
the side stories' finds. He is also the boat's angler, patient and lucky (his rod: "Jonah's rod", below). He was
eleven at the 1926 wedding and remembers it only as a day with cake.

**The younger brother: Kit, 13.** The user: "the younger boy 13 and eager and fresh blonde", the old-time sailor boy
outfit, the VR kid. Born 1921. Eager, quick, small enough that the old helmet fits him once padded with a rolled
scarf. He never dives in a suit: he goes below only as a fish, whatever he eats his way into, and scouts for his
brother. He was five at the wedding and does not remember it at all. He knows the family legend only as "Grandpa's
lost treasure".

**Why these three are on this boat.** A grandfather and his two grandsons for one summer, on the boat their mother
grew up on, chasing "Grandpa's lost treasure". The treasure is real (five chests, a music box, real gold beside it),
and it was laid for them, by their grandfather and the man he loves, as the way to tell them what neither old man
knows how to say aloud in 1934. The captain sails them to each place, works the pump, and waits for each photograph
to come up: the quiet tension of the whole game is an old man watching his grandsons learn who he is.

## What became of Vince (two versions; the user's call)

**Recommended: Vince is alive.** His lungs (forty years of diving) finished his diving in 1932; he lives ashore now,
in his sister Lucia's house above her fish market in Monterey, and mends nets in the back of the market. He is in the
background of every sale at Monterey, all game: an old man with a mandolin on a nail beside him and a medal at his
throat, who looks up when the boat comes in. The player does not know who he is until the end. The ending brings him
aboard.

**Alternative: Vince died in the winter of 1933,** and the hunt is his last gift; the ending is the captain going
below as a fish one last time, where a diver of light meets him (the first version's ending, kept below). The room
prefers the first: the user has not said he is dead; a long-delayed love that ends in a funeral is a worn path for
gay stories; and the version where the family brings him home is the warmer game.

## The lost treasure

**What it is.** A music box in five pieces, and the story it unlocks; real gold, silver and Atlantean pieces lie
beside it to be sold (Things to find).

- The case: oak and brass, the size of a loaf of bread (Monterey docks, a plain chest).
- The comb, the steel teeth that sing, faced with gold from a doubloon of the pirate ship (pirate hold, crowbar).
- The cylinder, pinned to one tune: the room proposes "The Water Is Wide" (a Scottish folk song, public domain, about
  love across water), which Mac played on the tin whistle and Vince on the mandolin on the Chicago's deck, half in a
  Neapolitan lilt (liner saloon, key ring).
- The lid: two small sailors carved in Atlantis's green stone, one stocky, one tall, who turn round each other on a
  turntable when it plays (Atlantis temple, lamp).
- The key that winds it (under the old one, the winch; with photograph 9).

**Whose it was, and why it matters.** It was made by Vince for Mac in 1926, their wedding present to each other, and
played every night in the cabin for six years. The two old men took it apart in 1932 and laid it, with twelve
photographs from the family album, across the places they had dived together, deepest last, so the boys would follow
it piece by piece and learn the story in order of courage rather than order of years. To the boys it is "Grandpa's
lost treasure". It turns out to be their grandfather's life.

**How the machine belongs.** Vince built it in 1927 and 1928 (an electrician and a diver: the honest root): so that Mac,
who could not swim, could come below with him at last. Mac always became the same fish, a giant sea bass (a
Californian giant, gentle, long-lived; the game has `sea_bass`). Every fish run is Kit using that gift again.

**How the monsters belong.** The old one sleeps in the canyon under Atlantis (side story C); the deepest chest is under
its chin because the last truth goes deepest. The leviathan, the ghosts, the mermaids and the submarine each have a
side story and touch the spine at one point.

## The shape of play

The photographs are found only on dives (the diver is Jonah), in the order the gear allows; Kit's fish runs and Jonah's
fishing earn the money for the gear. The five chests sit where DESIGN.md already puts them (docks, pirate hold, liner
saloon, Atlantis temple, under the old one), with two or three photographs near each.

**The album, emptied on purpose.** The family album, kept in the chart table's drawer. Its thirteen pages hold empty
photo corners: the twelve prints were taken out to lay the hunt. Under each frame Vince drew a little pencil sketch of
what goes there: a whistle and a mandolin, two figures in the water, a medal, a church door, a baby's candle, a cooking
pot, a boat's bow with a bottle, a covered pot on a wharf, two rings, a helmet, a sea lion, a mask head, and on the
thirteenth page, a boat with four figures. The sketches are the hints: no words, any age.

**The chart.** The captain marks each chest place on the chart with an X when the gear to reach it is bought (he knows
where they are; he laid them). After eleven photographs are in, he opens the album himself, stops at the ninth page's
sketch of two rings, sits a long moment, and draws the last X over the canyon floor (DESIGN.md: the last chest needs the
full album). The player sees him hesitate; the boys do not yet know why.

**How it reads as it is found.** Gear decides the order, roughly: the shallows first (the navy, the wedding, the
christening, the launch of the boat): "Grandpa and his best friend". Then the hard-hat places (Christmas in North
Beach, the helmet, the picnic): two old men having the time of their lives. Then the liner (the covered pot: comfort
after Ellen's death). Then Atlantis (the two of them below together). Last, the deep: the wedding. The boys (and the
player) come to it the way the two men did: slowly.

## The ending (wordless)

1. **The last dive.** The boat anchors over the canyon; the captain works the pump. Jonah goes down the shot line in
   the deep suit with the winch cable, to about 100 m. For the only time on a dive Kit comes too, as a fish (the
   amplifier's top step reaches 100 m), swimming at his brother's shoulder: a diver below, a fish beside him, a man at
   the pump, the picture of the two old men's own dives. The player is Jonah; Kit's fish follows. The old one's
   sleeping head fills the dark. Under its chin, a stone slab.
2. **The last chest.** Jonah winches the slab off; the old one stirs and settles. In the chest, a sealed tin: the music
   box key on a ribbon, and photograph 9, shown large: two old men in their best on the boat's deck, exchanging rings,
   Rose beside them holding a small boy by each hand. Jonah holds it close to his helmet's glass a long moment (the
   camera behind him). Kit's fish nudges his glass. He tugs the line.
3. **On deck.** Jonah, helmet off, does not hand the tin over. He looks at his grandfather, then puts his hand on the
   old man's shoulder. Kit comes out of the cabin, helmet in his arms, and the tin goes to him; he looks at it and
   throws his arms round his grandfather. No words. The captain goes into the cabin.
4. **Coming home.** He comes out in the navy double-breasted coat with the red carnation (the outfit sheet's): the coat
   in photograph 9. He takes the wheel. The boat steams into Monterey at dusk (the player can steer it in, if the
   sailing idea the user mentioned is built; otherwise it is a short scene). On the end of the wharf, an old man with a
   cane and a mandolin case waits: the man from the back of the Monterey market. Jonah throws him a line; Kit hands him
   down onto the deck.
5. **The final image.** The music box, whole, on the hatch cover, plays. Vince sits beside Mac; he takes the mandolin
   out and plays along, badly, and Mac laughs. The game's camera takes a photograph: the four on the stern in the last
   light. It freezes, tones to sepia, takes a deckled edge, and drops into the album's thirteenth page under Vince's
   sketch of a boat with four figures. The album closes.

After the ending the game goes on (fish runs, dives, fishing), and Vince is aboard on the title from then on: sitting by
the machine, or with Kit at the console.

## How the rest touches the story

- **Mac's outfits.** Working clothes (open navy coat over a striped shirt, the yellow oilskins) every day. The cream
  cable-knit is Ellen's knitting and the dark scarf is his mother's tartan: cold mornings. Bare-chested in braces and a
  bandana: hot work, the tattoos showing. The striped bathing suit with the towel: a family joke (he sunbathes on deck
  and never goes in). The navy double-breasted coat with the white cap and red carnation: his wedding coat of 1926,
  worn again at the ending.
- **The tattoos** (the build script's flash: anchor, swallow, compass star, a ship on a ribbon, a heart, no letters):
  sailors' swallows, the pair to Vince's, got in Villefranche on the 1890 cruise (photograph 1's detail); the ship on
  the ribbon is the Chicago; the anchor is the navy; the compass star the way home; the heart with nothing in its
  ribbon is the name a man of his time could not write.
- **The saint's medal**: Vince's, given to Mac at their parting in 1892 (photograph 3), worn by Mac under his shirt
  for thirty-four years and given back on their wedding day (photograph 9 shows it at Vince's throat again).
- **The old one** sleeps over the last chest; the Atlanteans' lullaby keeps it asleep (side story C).
- **The leviathan** is a harmless old giant scarred by harpoons (side story F): big is not cruel.
- **The ghosts** gather where music plays.
- **The liner** sank in 1903 in fog off Point Lobos; Vince was the diver who brought up its dead's keepsakes for their
  families (side story A).
- **The pirate ship** gave the gold for the comb. **Atlantis** gave the green stone of the dancers.
- **The submarine** is a naturalist's (side story G); one sketch shows an old diver beside a giant sea bass.
- **The navy:** the mines in the canyon's head are a 1918 navy field never cleared (danger, not story); Mac's old
  sea bag hangs in the cabin with the Chicago's name stencilled on it as a shape (a cruiser's silhouette).
- **The markets:** Monterey's is Vince's sister Lucia's (Vince in the back); Santa Cruz's the old angler's (side story
  D); Carmel's Rose's.

## Tone

A love story between two men across a whole life, told with dignity and warmth: a youth where love could not be
spoken, a long friendship that was itself a kind of love, a real marriage honoured, grief met with a covered pot of
stew, and joy late. Nobody in it is a villain or a joke. Humour lives in the things (the colander helmet, the swim
call, the sea lion, the mandolin played badly). Suitable for a broad arcade audience: nothing beyond a hand on a
shoulder, an arm round a back, rings, and two small sailors turning on a music box.

## What each shows, and what the player understands

**1. Music on the deck (1889).** The Chicago's white-painted deck by a gun, at sea, bright. Two young sailors in navy
whites sit on a hatch: Mac (red-brown hair, broad) with a tin whistle, Vince (dark, lean, moustache) with a mandolin,
playing, leaning toward each other. *Detail:* fresh swallow tattoos on both forearms, side by side. *Understood:* Grandpa
was a sailor; this is his best friend.

**2. Swim call (1890).** The cruiser's side, a boat boom out over the sea, swim call. Vince in the water holding up both
hands; Mac on the boom ladder in his drawers, stiff as a post, gripping the rope, refusing. Sailors laughing on the rail
above. *Detail:* Mac's white knuckles. *Understood:* Grandpa can't swim (still can't). Funny.

**3. The medal (1892).** The navy dock at Mare Island, grey morning, a brick store and a gangway. Mac in a civilian coat,
sea bag on his shoulder: discharged. Vince in uniform has just put his saint's medal round Mac's neck; their hands are
clasped, and neither has let go. *Detail:* the handshake held too long. *Understood:* a parting that cost both of them.

**4. The best man (1895).** A studio wedding portrait: Ellen seated in a high-collared dress with a bouquet, Mac
standing beside her with a hand on her shoulder, both upright and proud; a little apart, Vince in navy dress blues as
best man, hands behind his back. *Detail:* Vince is looking at the couple, not the camera. *Understood:* Grandpa married
Grandma, and his friend stood by him. (Ellen is shown as a bride and a person, not a contrast: the picture is happy.)

**5. The godfather (1896).** A studio christening portrait: Vince in uniform holding the baby in a long christening
gown; Ellen and Mac either side, Ellen's hand on the baby. *Detail:* the baby (Mom). *Understood:* Vince is Mom's
godfather; he was family.

**6. Christmas in North Beach (1910).** A kitchen: Vince's mother at the stove with a great pot of cioppino; Ellen
beside her with a ladle; Rose at fourteen at the table; Mac in his mother's tartan scarf; Vince in his chief petty

Christmases.

**7. The launch (1914).** This very shrimp boat, new and clean, at the Monterey commercial wharf. Ellen on the wharf
with a bottle to break on the bow; Rose beside her; Mac at the wheelhouse door; Vince on deck in his salvage diver's

**8. The covered pot (1925).** Winter, the boat at the wharf. Mac alone on the stern in a black armband, sitting with

**10. The helmet (1928).** The boat's cabin, lamp-lit: the machine's first version on the bench. Mac seated in a crude

**11. The picnic on Seal Rock (1930).** The flat rock among the stacks, golden hour, from behind: two old men side by side

**12. Below together (1932).** Atlantis in the dark, one lamp. Vince, old, in the armoured suit, the lamp held high on

**13. The four of them (1934, at the ending).** The stern at dusk: Mac in the carnation coat and Vince with the mandolin

## Finding order and telling order

The shallows give the youth and the family years first, which read as friendship; the hard-hat places give the old men's
happiness; the liner gives the grief between; Atlantis the two below together; the deep gives the wedding. The album
lays them out by year, so a player who has found 8, 10, 11 and 12 sees an empty page 9 between grief and joy, with a
sketch of two rings. **Photograph 9 is found last and changes the others:** the "best friend" becomes the man Grandpa
married; the best-man picture, the medal, the covered pot all turn into chapters of one love; the boys in the picture
are the boys holding it; and the empty album was the old men's way of saying it.

# PART THREE: STAGING BRIEFS

## The common recipe

- **Tooling (proposal):** `tools/photo_stage.gd` loads a named scene (place, figures, outfits, props, a pose per bone
  group, the camera), renders 1600x1200 (or upright 1200x1600), saves the raw picture; one sepia pass for all thirteen,
  the last applied live at the ending.
- **The sepia pass:** desaturate, map to a warm umber-to-cream ramp, lift the blacks, soft vignette, fine grain, two or
  three hairline scratches and dust by seed, slight corner blur; a deckled cream border for land pictures; a dark round
  porthole mask for underwater ones (12); damage by state (tide marks for loose, a crack for framed, foxing for bottled).
  Older pictures (1 to 5) take a browner ramp and more fading than the later ones (8 to 12), so the album visibly ages
  from front to back.
- **Lens:** 35 to 50 mm equivalent, chest height, level horizon, people posed and still. Studio portraits (4, 5) square
  on, figures in a row, as nineteenth-century studios did.
- **Faces:** nothing depends on a face; each picture reads by its one detail. With stylised faces, closed-mouth smiles at
  most (old photographs were not grinning), except 6, 11 and 13 where laughter is the point and is carried by heads
  thrown back.

# PART FOUR: OTHER STORIES

The captain's story is the spine; these are side stories the sea tells, each found through things on the floor and
readable without words. Each is a row on the cabin's curio shelf with a painted silhouette for each missing piece.
Completing one gives a small thing, never anything the spine needs. Gear steps are DESIGN.md's (canvas 10 m, hard-hat

### Jonah's rod (how the fishing belongs)

Jonah is the boat's angler: the calm, steady earner. His catches go to the same three markets as Kit's fish runs, and
each market's wall puts up the first of each kind he lands. What he lands at an anchorage hints at the floor: a fish
caught over a wreck sometimes comes up with something snagged (a doubloon, an old lure, a pearl), which marks the chart.
His big quarry is the old angler's lingcod (D). At the ending he lays the rod down on the rail to throw Vince a line.
*Outfits* (concept first): a working one (oilskin smock over a striped jersey, rolled trousers, sea boots, a knitted
watch cap over the curls) and a fair-weather one (rolled shirt sleeves, braces, a straw hat, bare feet).

### A. The liner's last night (the liner off Point Lobos, Carmel)

The liner struck the point in fog in 1903 while its band played to keep the passengers calm. *Gear:* hard-hat, key ring
for the cabins, armoured for the low side. *Finds (6):* the ship's bell, clapper tied with a bandsman's scarf; the
bandmaster's cornet; a pocket watch stopped at ten past two; a silver tray with the line's crest (two waves and a star);
a child's porcelain doll in the empty nursery; a lifeboat's bow plate, far off toward Carmel. *Understood:* the band
played, the boats got away (the doll's owner too: the shelf card is a girl on Carmel beach holding a new doll). Vince
was the diver who brought keepsakes up for families that year; his navy diving knife lies in the saloon where he lost
it. *Completing gives:* the ship's bell on the boat; it rings when a catch is sold. *Touches the spine:* the cylinder's
chest and photograph 8 lie in the saloon, where Vince had worked.

### B. The pirate ship's crew (the pirate ship and Seal Rock)

The "pirates" were runaway ship's children. *Gear:* hard-hat, crowbar. *Finds (6):* a tricorn far too small for a man;

## PART ONE: THE STORY (for builders)

### The story in five sentences

Forty years ago, in the 1890s, a young Monterey deckhand who could not swim fell in love with a diver who built
machines, and the diver built him a helmet that let his mind swim beside him as a fish. Together they found the
pirate ship, the liner and Atlantis, pulled a baby girl out of the sea the night the liner went down and raised her
as their daughter, and the diver set out to lay a treasure hunt across the bay as a proposal, five chests that made a
music box, each with photographs of their life. He was lost laying the last chest, under the sleeping old one in the
canyon, and the captain raised their girl alone and never went below again. Now the captain is a grandfather, and his
two grandsons have come aboard for the summer: the older one walks the floor in the diver's old suit (and fishes
from the rail), the younger one swims as a fish in the old helmet, the old man works the pump again, and between
them they follow the hunt, bringing the photographs up one by one into an album the diver had emptied on purpose. The
last chest holds the ring and the last photograph, the question asked forty years late, and the captain answers it: he puts on his best coat and the old helmet and goes below for the first time since, and the
game ends on a new photograph of the old man and his grandsons.

### The people

All names are proposals; the game shows almost no words, so a name matters only to builders and to files.

**The captain: Hollis ("Holl") Marr, about 60 in the game's present (1934).** The user's: "gay captain", "bushy chest
hair and tattoos", "staunch proportions", the boys' "gay grandpa". He has skippered the same shrimp boat for nearly
forty years. Stocky, slow, kind, funny without trying; a man who expresses everything through his hands and his
work. He cannot swim, and never learned (plenty of old sailors could not): this is the joke of photograph 3 and the
weight of the ending. He has not worn the helmet since the day the diver was lost, and he lets his grandson wear it
now: that he allows it at all is the first step of the story, shown and never said.

**The man he loved: Teodoro ("Teo") Bellini, about 26 in the photographs (1894 to 1898).** A salvage diver on the
Monterey wharves, the son of a Sicilian fishing family (Monterey's fishing families were largely Sicilian and
Portuguese then). Tall and lean against Hollis's barrel, with a big 1890s moustache and round wire spectacles
(both read on a mask face, and give a stylised face a clear identity), a flat cap on land. A tinkerer with a net-loft
workshop on Cannery Row, half diver, half mad inventor: he built the machine. Playful, restless, romantic: the
treasure hunt is his idea of a proposal, since in those years two men could not marry and he wanted to ask anyway.
He was lost in 1898 at 30. His body was never found; his empty deep suit is found at the end.

**Their daughter, the boys' mother: Marina, about 39 in the present.** The answer to how a gay grandfather has
grandsons, given truthfully: she was a baby nobody claimed, pulled from the water the night the liner struck Point
Lobos in 1895 and taken home by the two young men who pulled her out. They raised her together for three years and
Hollis raised her alone after that. She grew up on the boat. She is alive and well: she runs the fish market at
Carmel, the town nearest the wreck she came from, with her husband (who can stay off screen). She has sent her sons
to their grandfather for the summer. She appears in the photographs as a baby and a toddler, and in the present only
as a figure behind the Carmel market's counter if a builder wants her there.

**The older brother, the diver and the angler: Jonah, 19.** The user: "a budding handsom curly dark haired sailor

### The lost treasure

**What it is.** A music box, in five pieces, made by Teo for Hollis, and the two rings that go with it.

- The case: a small sea chest of oak and brass, the size of a loaf of bread.
- The comb, the tuned steel teeth that sing, and the two rings, all three cut from one gold doubloon out of the
  pirate ship (photograph 6).
- The cylinder, its pins set to one tune: the lullaby Teo copied from the carvings of Atlantis.
- The lid: two small figures carved in Atlantean green stone, a stocky one and a tall thin one in spectacles, who
  turn round each other on a little turntable when it plays.
- The key that winds it, with Teo's ring tied to it on a ribbon, in the last chest with the last photograph.

**Whose it was, and why it matters.** Teo's, for Hollis: a proposal laid out as a treasure hunt, each chest a little
deeper than the last, so that the man who could not swim would have to come further each time (as a fish, in the
helmet Teo had built for him). Teo meant to lead him to the last chest himself. To the boys it is "Grandpa's lost
treasure"; it turns out to be the question their grandfather was never asked.

**How it was lost.** Teo laid the chests over a summer in 1898, telling Hollis they were salvage jobs, and took the
photographs out of their album to lay with them. On the last dive, in the deep suit, to the canyon floor under the
old one, the old one turned in its sleep and its fin fouled the hose; Hollis, at the pump, hauled up a cut hose end.
That hose end hangs coiled on a nail in the boat's cabin to this day (an object in the cabin: it is never explained;
a player who has found photograph 12 understands it).

**Is there gold too?** Yes, for the game's loop: coins, the liner's silver, Atlantean pieces and more lie on the floor
and in the chests beside the music box's pieces, and are sold (Things to find, below). The music box is never sold.

**How the machine, the fish and the monsters belong to the same story.**

- The machine is Teo's: he built it so the man who could not swim could come below with him. Every fish run in the
  game is that gift being used again. The static at the machine's depth limit is just the limit; the room does not
  propose that it hides a voice.
- The fish: Hollis, in the helmet, always became the same kind: a young giant sea bass, spotted and orange as the young
  are (a Californian giant that lives for seventy years and is gentle; Gulp has `sea_bass` in its tree). At the end he
  becomes one again, grey and enormous now.
- The old one sleeps in the canyon under Atlantis. It is not evil: it is asleep, and the city of Atlantis was built on
  its shoulder by people who sang it to sleep (other stories, C). Teo's death was an accident of its turning over, not
  an attack. The music box plays the Atlanteans' lullaby: on the last dive it is what lets Jonah work under its chin.
- The leviathan, the ghosts, the mermaids, the submarine: each has its own side story (below); each touches the
  spine at one point.

### Tone

A love story between two men told with dignity and warmth: their life is shown as ordinary and good (work, jokes, a
picnic, a baby, a tattoo parlour), not as a secret or a punishment. The loss happened long ago and is shown by an
absence (an empty space beside a kneeling diver, a cut hose on a nail), never by a body or a scene of dying. The
story's present is about a grandfather, his grandsons and a question finally answered. There is room for the user's
humour throughout: the captain in a colander helmet, the swim lesson, the coin-biting, the sea lion stealing a
photograph, the river beast's hoard of hats. Suitable for a broad arcade audience: nothing in it is more than a
held hand, an arm round a shoulder, two figures turning on a music box, a kneeling man with a ring.

## PART THREE: STAGING BRIEFS (making the photographs in the game)

### The common recipe

Every photograph is a scene built from the game's own models, posed, and taken by the game's camera; then toned.

- **Tooling (proposal):** one `tools/photo_stage.gd` that loads a named scene (place, figures, outfits, props, a pose
  per bone group, the camera), renders at 1600x1200 (4:3, the shape of a cabinet card) or 1200x1600 for upright ones,
  and saves the raw picture; then one sepia pass (a post shader or a small image script), applied the same to all
  thirteen, the last one in real time at the ending.

## PART FOUR: OTHER STORIES

The captain's story is the spine; these are side stories the sea tells, each found through things on the floor and
readable without words. Each is a set of finds that go to the cabin's shelf (Things to find, below) in a row of their
own, with an empty silhouette for each missing piece, the same rule as the album. Completing a set gives one small
thing, never anything the spine needs. Gear steps refer to DESIGN.md (canvas 10 m, hard-hat 25 m, armoured 50 m, deep
100 m; lamp, crowbar, key ring, winch).

#### Jonah's rod (how the fishing belongs to the story)

The user: "the older brother is the one who does the regular fishing". Jonah is the boat's angler, and the room
proposes his fishing carries three small threads, none a new system:

- **The money.** Fishing from the rail is the calm, steady earner (DESIGN.md); Jonah's catches go to the same three
  markets as Kit's fish runs. Each market's wall gets the first of each kind he lands as well (a trophy on a board).
- **The angler's eye.** What he lands at an anchorage shows that place's life before he dives it: a fish he catches
  over the pirate ship sometimes comes up with something in its gut or snagged on the hook (a doubloon, a lure from side
  story D, a pearl in an abalone fragment), a small find that marks the chart: the rod as a hint to where to walk.
- **The one that got away** (side story D): the giant lingcod is his to land.
- **At the ending** he sets the rod down on the rail to take the pump: the angler does not fish that evening.

#### A. The liner's last night (the liner off Point Lobos, Carmel)

The liner struck the point in fog on a night of music, 1895. *Gear:* hard-hat for the upper decks, key ring for the
cabins, armoured for the engine room's low side. *Finds (6):* the ship's bell, its clapper tied with a bandsman's
scarf (rang no alarm: the band had been told to keep playing to keep the passengers calm); the bandmaster's cornet in
the saloon; a pocket watch stopped at ten past two; a silver serving tray with the line's crest (two waves and a
star: a crest, no words); the nursery's empty cradle (where photograph 9 lies); a lifeboat's bow plate with a painted
number, on the sand far from the wreck, toward Carmel. *Understood:* the ship went down while the band played; the
lifeboats got away, one plate drifted ashore at Carmel; one baby was missed in the nursery. *Completing gives:* the
ship's bell, hung on the shrimp boat; it rings when a catch is sold. *Touches the spine:* it is where Marina came from;
the lifebelt on Carmel market's wall is hers.

#### B. The pirate ship's crew (the pirate ship and Seal Rock)

The "pirates" were runaway children. *Gear:* hard-hat, crowbar. *Finds (6):* a captain's tricorn far too small for a
man; a carved wooden toy whale; a cracked spyglass; a slate with a chalk drawing in a child's hand (a big man with a
whip, small figures running onto a ship, the ship sailing off: picture-writing); the figurehead's lost arm, in the
sand; on Seal Rock itself, small handprints and tally marks scratched into the stone above the waterline. *Understood:*
a crew of ship's boys and girls took their cruel master's ship and sailed it away; it struck the outcrop, and they all
got ashore on the seals' rock and lived there till they were found. *Completing gives:* the figurehead's arm put back
on the mermaid figurehead: she points down into the hold, where the crowbar chest is (a key to the chest the spine
needs; the chest can still be found without it). *Touches the spine:* their gold became the rings; children sailing
off on their own are Kit and Jonah's mirror.

#### C. The lullaby of Atlantis (the ruins in the lower canyon)

Who built Atlantis, and what sleeps under it. *Gear:* armoured suit, lamp. *Finds (5):* four stone tablets of
picture-writing (masked people building a city on a mountain that breathes; the mountain opening an eye; the people
playing lyres and horns, the eye closing; the people walking into the sea and growing tails), and a stone lyre.
*Understood:* the city was built on the shoulder of the sleeping old one, and its people sang it to sleep for
generations; when they went into the sea (the mask-faced mermaids are their children), the giant rolled in its sleep
and the city slid into the canyon. *Completing gives:* the tune itself, which Teo pinned on the music box's cylinder
(so the box plays it once the set is done; before that the box is silent: a reason to finish C before the end,
not a lock on it). *Touches the spine:* the lullaby lets Jonah work under the old one's chin; the dancers on the lid are
carved from Atlantis's green stone.

#### D. The one that got away (Santa Cruz wharf, the sand flats)

The fishermen's story, with the user's humour. An old angler has fished off the end of the Santa Cruz wharf every day
for forty years for one huge old lingcod, and runs the Santa Cruz market now with his son. *Gear:* canvas suit for the
wharf, hard-hat for the sand flats; the rod for the end. *Finds (6):* his lures, one per decade, lost in the sand, each
a different style (a wooden plug, a spoon, a feather jig...); his pipe; his hat; the snapped end of his best rod.
*Understood:* the same fish has broken him off for forty years. *The end:* the lingcod lives in the sand flats with
all six lures' hooks in its lip; Jonah, who finds the lures on his dives, can hook it from the rail with his rod once
all six are found (the fishing mode's one named fish, and the hardest fight in it). It is not sold: he brings it in, and the photograph-card for the shelf shows
the old man at the market holding it up, then letting it go off the wharf. *Completing gives:* the best tackle step
in the shop, cheaper. *Touches the spine:* he knew Hollis and Teo (he is a figure in the background of photograph 2,
fishing off the wharf, if a builder wants the nod).

#### E. The beast of the slough (the mangrove river)

The furry crocodile-headed river beast (the user's own creature, made in that spirit, not a copy of the drawing) has
been stealing from fishermen for longer than anyone remembers. *Gear:* canvas suit; the lamp helps under the roots.
*Finds (6):* a heap of stolen soft things found round its den in the mangrove roots: a fisherman's sou'wester, a
child's knitted hat, a boot, a lunch pail, a bandana, a woollen sock. And photograph 8 in its cracked frame, used as a
lid. *Understood:* it is a mother lining a nest with warm things; three eggs lie in it. *The end:* when the eggs hatch
(after the set is complete, the next visit), the young follow the boat's wake up the river once and go back. *Completing
gives:* nothing to sell; the den's eggs become the shelf's set picture. *Touches the spine:* it took photograph 8, which
washed into the slough.

#### F. The leviathan's harpoons (open ocean and the canyon's basin)

The leviathan is a harmless old giant (the user's "leviathan", built as one). *Gear:* hard-hat for the open-water
floor at the edge of the shelf, armoured for the basin. *Finds (5):* four harpoon heads of four different ages lying
along its path (each older one more rusted: a bone-and-iron one, a hand iron, a toggle iron, a bomb-lance), and a
whale's tooth with scrimshaw (a whaling ship and a great beast rising under it). Also on the floor, the whaler's
broken whaleboat. *Understood:* it has been hunted for a hundred years and has turned on its hunters only once;
it means no harm. *Completing gives:* the scrimshaw on the shelf; the leviathan lets the player's fish ride its
slipstream (if a builder wants a reward in play; otherwise nothing). *Touches the spine:* big is not the same as cruel,
the lesson the old one needs.

#### G. The iron fish (the submarine, on the canyon wall past the shelf's edge)

A 1910s submarine, nose-down where it grounded. Its owner was a naturalist who drew everything she saw. *Gear:*
armoured suit, key ring for its hatch, lamp. *Finds (6):* her sketchbook in a watertight case (its plates are the
game's own creatures drawn in ink: the evolution tree's art); three specimen jars (a lanternfish, a firefly squid, a
baby anglerfish); her pocket compass; the escape buoy's cut line at the open top hatch. *Understood:* she got out, by
the buoy, and lived; the drawings are her life's work. *Completing gives:* the evolution tree's pages, drawn in her
hand (a reskin of the tree screen, not a new system). *Touches the spine:* one sketch shows a diver in spectacles
beside a spotted sea bass: she saw them once.

#### H. Jonah's pearls (kelp, rocks, the outcrop)

The diver brother's own reason for being here. Jonah is saving pearls for someone on the Santa Cruz boardwalk (seen
only as a lit window and a figure who waves from the wharf when the boat passes; the room proposes leaving who it is
unshown). *Gear:* canvas suit for the kelp; hard-hat for the outcrop. *Finds (6):* abalone pearls (California's own:
baroque, green-blue), each in an abalone shell on the rocks; he prises them off himself and they go to his corner
of the shelf, not to market.
*Understood:* the big brother is courting. *Completing gives:* a sepia card for the shelf (Jonah at the boardwalk
rail, someone's hand in his) and his own work outfit changes to his best (a clean jersey) on the title.

## APPENDIX: HOW THE ROOM GOT HERE

### The three treatments

#### Treatment 1: "The Proposal" (grounded; chosen, then blended)

A young deckhand who cannot swim and a diver who builds machines fall in love on the Monterey wharves in the 1890s.
The diver builds a helmet so his love can swim beside him as a fish; together they find the pirate ship, the liner
and Atlantis. The diver lays a treasure hunt across the bay, five chests that make a music box, with photographs of
their life laid along the way, as a proposal. He is lost laying the last chest under the sleeping old one. Forty years
on the captain, with his grandsons aboard, lets the younger one wear the helmet; the boys follow the hunt without
knowing what it is. The last chest holds the ring and a photograph of the diver kneeling with it, beside an empty
space. The captain puts on his best coat, wears the helmet, and goes below as a fish one last time; a photograph of
the three of them fills the album's last page.

#### Treatment 2: "The Man from the Sea" (mythic)

The man the captain loved came out of the sea: one of the mask-faced people of Atlantis, who could walk on land only
for a season each year. Every photograph is of a summer they had. The treasure is the crown of Atlantis, which lets
its wearer stay ashore; the lover broke it into pieces and hid them so he could not be taken back. The boy, as fish
and diver, gathers the crown; at the end the captain must choose to give it back; he walks into the sea with his
lover, who has come up at the music of the box, and the boys see two mask-faced figures go down into the light.

#### Treatment 3: "The Signal" (mad science)

The captain's love built the machine and, dying in a diving accident, put his mind into the sea through it: he is
the static at the machine's depth limit, a voice in the noise. The photographs are his memories, scattered by the
signal as it frayed. The boy gathers them to rebuild his mind; the gear ladder is the amplifier reaching him. At the
end the signal is clear: the lover's mind is in an old whale, and the captain puts on the helmet and swims with him.

### The room's criticism

**Treatment 1.**
- *Story editor:* moves well: every chest is a step and the last one turns everything round (the photographs were
  laid, not lost). The ending lands because it is an answer and not only a loss: the old man does the one thing he
  could never do. Risk: a dead lover is a familiar trope for gay stories; the room answers it by setting the death
  forty years back, never showing it, and making the present about family, an answer and joy.
- *Game designer:* every beat sits on a place DESIGN.md already has, a gear step it already sells and a chest it
  already plans; the photographs give each dive a reason; the album's pencil sketches are a wordless quest log; the
  ending reuses the fish run with its systems off. Tellable with no dialogue: yes, through objects.
- *The user's taste:* closest to the user's own painting *Finding* (a diver on the reef before a wreck: "Well, I've
  never really found what I was looking for anyways...") and *Red Rabbit* ("I was just a boy when I found you"): a
  search whose treasure is a person. Warm and funny, as their Eddie and Owen pictures are.

**Treatment 2.**
- *Story editor:* a strong fairy-tale shape, but the ending is the captain leaving his grandsons: sad for a family
  game, and it reads as death.
- *Game designer:* the crown is a good collectible; a lover who comes and goes by season has nowhere to stand in a
  game set in one summer; the walk into the sea needs a new scene with no play in it.
- *The user's taste:* uses the mask faces and Atlantis well (the Atlantis concept's statue and the mermaid concept
  both have the figures' mask face), and the liminal Other World the user named for Warren's lore. Kept: the
  Atlanteans and mermaids as side story C.

**Treatment 3.**
- *Story editor:* clever, but explains too much and too strangely; the photographs scattered by a signal makes no
  physical sense, and the ending depends on a rule (minds in whales) the player has to be told.
- *Game designer:* the static at the depth limit and the amplifier as the way to a voice is the neatest fit of gear to
  story of the three; but a voice needs words, and the game has almost none.
- *The user's taste:* the mad-science machine as an act of love is very theirs (the old machines, The Simulatrix's lab,
  the Frankenstein panel). Kept: the machine built as a gift; dropped: the trapped mind.

**Choice:** Treatment 1, with the machine as a love gift from 3, and the Atlanteans and mermaids of 2 as a side story
and the source of the lullaby. Then reshaped for the user's later words: the captain is the boys' grandfather (so the
liner gives the couple their daughter), and the other stories and finds were added.
