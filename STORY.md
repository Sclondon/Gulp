# PART ONE: THE STORY (for builders)

## The story in five sentences

Forty years ago, in the 1890s, a young Monterey deckhand who could not swim fell in love with a diver who built
machines, and the diver built him a helmet that let his mind swim beside him as a fish. Together they found the
pirate ship, the liner and Atlantis, pulled a baby girl out of the sea the night the liner went down and raised her
as their daughter, and the diver set out to lay a treasure hunt across the bay as a proposal, five chests that made a
music box, each with photographs of their life. He was lost laying the last chest, under the sleeping old one in the
canyon, and the captain raised their girl alone and never went below again. Now the captain is a grandfather, and his
two grandsons have come aboard for the summer: the older one walks the floor in the diver's old suit (and fishes
from the rail), the younger one swims as a fish in the old helmet, the old man works the pump again, and between
them they follow the hunt, bringing the photographs up one by one into an album the diver had emptied on purpose. The
last chest holds the ring and the last photograph, the question asked forty years late, and the captain answers it: he puts on his best coat and the old helmet and goes below for the first time since, and the
game ends on a new photograph of the old man and his grandsons.

## The people

All names are proposals; the game shows almost no words, so a name matters only to builders and to files.

**The captain: Hollis ("Holl") Marr, about 60 in the game's present (1934).** The user's: "gay captain", "bushy chest
hair and tattoos", "staunch proportions", the boys' "gay grandpa". He has skippered the same shrimp boat for nearly
forty years. Stocky, slow, kind, funny without trying; a man who expresses everything through his hands and his
work. He cannot swim, and never learned (plenty of old sailors could not): this is the joke of photograph 3 and the
weight of the ending. He has not worn the helmet since the day the diver was lost, and he lets his grandson wear it
now: that he allows it at all is the first step of the story, shown and never said.

**The man he loved: Teodoro ("Teo") Bellini, about 26 in the photographs (1894 to 1898).** A salvage diver on the
Monterey wharves, the son of a Sicilian fishing family (Monterey's fishing families were largely Sicilian and
Portuguese then). Tall and lean against Hollis's barrel, with a big 1890s moustache and round wire spectacles
(both read on a mask face, and give a stylised face a clear identity), a flat cap on land. A tinkerer with a net-loft
workshop on Cannery Row, half diver, half mad inventor: he built the machine. Playful, restless, romantic: the
treasure hunt is his idea of a proposal, since in those years two men could not marry and he wanted to ask anyway.
He was lost in 1898 at 30. His body was never found; his empty deep suit is found at the end.

**Their daughter, the boys' mother: Marina, about 39 in the present.** The answer to how a gay grandfather has
grandsons, given truthfully: she was a baby nobody claimed, pulled from the water the night the liner struck Point
Lobos in 1895 and taken home by the two young men who pulled her out. They raised her together for three years and
Hollis raised her alone after that. She grew up on the boat. She is alive and well: she runs the fish market at
Carmel, the town nearest the wreck she came from, with her husband (who can stay off screen). She has sent her sons
to their grandfather for the summer. She appears in the photographs as a baby and a toddler, and in the present only
as a figure behind the Carmel market's counter if a builder wants her there.

**Three ways to play, one each** (the user's, 2026-11-18): the younger boy is the fish, through the machine and its
helmet; the older brother goes down in the diving suit and walks the floor ("no the older brohter is the walkerin the
diving suitt bruh"), and he is also the one who fishes with rod and line ("the older brother is the one who does the
regular fishing so he needs outfits for that"); the captain sails. Where DESIGN.md says "the boy" in the diving suit,
read the older brother. Every dive, find and staging brief below follows this.

**The older brother, the diver and the angler: Jonah, 19.** The user: "a budding handsom curly dark haired sailor
about 19", "the diver is the older brother to the vr kid". He wants to be what Teo was. His grandfather taught him to
dive and works his pump and hose from the deck, exactly as he did for Teo forty years ago: the same picture, a
generation on, and the reason the old man can bear the dives at all is that his own hands are on the hose. Jonah wears
Teo's old hard-hat suit, refitted (the diver on the title is in it), then the better suits the shop sells. **He is the
one who finds everything on the floor:** the photographs, the chests, the side stories' finds. He is also the boat's
angler: patient, lucky and proud of it, the opposite of his fidgety little brother. Between dives he fishes from the
rail with his rod (his outfits for it: Things to find and the needs list). What he fishes for: whatever sells (the
everyday money of the game), the old angler's giant lingcod (side story D), and once, at the ending, nothing at all.
His own story is his pearls (side story H).

**The boy, the fish: Kit, 13.** The user: "the younger boy 13 and eager and fresh blonde", the old-time sailor boy
outfit, the VR kid. Eager, quick, small enough that the old helmet fits him (it was made for an adult's head and
padded for him with a rolled scarf: a detail, not a word). He never dives in a suit: he goes below only as a fish,
whatever he eats his way into, and he scouts for his brother: a fish run shows the floor that Jonah will walk (what a
fish sees glinting goes onto the chart as a mark, if a builder wants that link; the room's proposal). He knows only the
family legend: "Grandpa's treasure" (Teo's name is not a word anyone says on screen).

**Who they are to each other, and why these three are on this boat.** A grandfather and his two grandsons, for one
summer, on the boat their mother grew up on. The boys came for the summer and the treasure legend; the grandfather
agreed because the boys are old enough, and because Jonah in Teo's suit on the floor, Kit in the helmet as a fish,
and the old man's own hands on the pump make up again the picture of the life he lost: a diver below, a fish beside
him, someone at the pump. He can finally bear to go back to it.

## The lost treasure

**What it is.** A music box, in five pieces, made by Teo for Hollis, and the two rings that go with it.

- The case: a small sea chest of oak and brass, the size of a loaf of bread.
- The comb, the tuned steel teeth that sing, and the two rings, all three cut from one gold doubloon out of the
  pirate ship (photograph 6).
- The cylinder, its pins set to one tune: the lullaby Teo copied from the carvings of Atlantis.
- The lid: two small figures carved in Atlantean green stone, a stocky one and a tall thin one in spectacles, who
  turn round each other on a little turntable when it plays.
- The key that winds it, with Teo's ring tied to it on a ribbon, in the last chest with the last photograph.

**Whose it was, and why it matters.** Teo's, for Hollis: a proposal laid out as a treasure hunt, each chest a little
deeper than the last, so that the man who could not swim would have to come further each time (as a fish, in the
helmet Teo had built for him). Teo meant to lead him to the last chest himself. To the boys it is "Grandpa's lost
treasure"; it turns out to be the question their grandfather was never asked.

**How it was lost.** Teo laid the chests over a summer in 1898, telling Hollis they were salvage jobs, and took the
photographs out of their album to lay with them. On the last dive, in the deep suit, to the canyon floor under the
old one, the old one turned in its sleep and its fin fouled the hose; Hollis, at the pump, hauled up a cut hose end.
That hose end hangs coiled on a nail in the boat's cabin to this day (an object in the cabin: it is never explained;
a player who has found photograph 12 understands it).

**Is there gold too?** Yes, for the game's loop: coins, the liner's silver, Atlantean pieces and more lie on the floor
and in the chests beside the music box's pieces, and are sold (Things to find, below). The music box is never sold.

**How the machine, the fish and the monsters belong to the same story.**

- The machine is Teo's: he built it so the man who could not swim could come below with him. Every fish run in the
  game is that gift being used again. The static at the machine's depth limit is just the limit; the room does not
  propose that it hides a voice.
- The fish: Hollis, in the helmet, always became the same kind: a young giant sea bass, spotted and orange as the young
  are (a Californian giant that lives for seventy years and is gentle; Gulp has `sea_bass` in its tree). At the end he
  becomes one again, grey and enormous now.
- The old one sleeps in the canyon under Atlantis. It is not evil: it is asleep, and the city of Atlantis was built on
  its shoulder by people who sang it to sleep (other stories, C). Teo's death was an accident of its turning over, not
  an attack. The music box plays the Atlanteans' lullaby: on the last dive it is what lets Jonah work under its chin.
- The leviathan, the ghosts, the mermaids, the submarine: each has its own side story (below); each touches the
  spine at one point.

## The shape of play

The photographs are found only as the diver (the user's words; the diver is Jonah), in the order the gear allows,
while Kit's fish runs earn the money for that gear and the fishing from the rail earns the rest; the album at the chart
table lays them out in the order they were taken; the five music-box pieces sit in the five chests DESIGN.md already
names (docks, pirate hold, liner saloon, Atlantis temple, under the old one). Each chest place has two or three
photographs nearby. The album is the quest log and has no words:

**The album, emptied on purpose.** It is Teo's, kept by Hollis for forty years in the chart table's drawer. Its
thirteen pages each have four photo corners and nothing in them, because Teo took the prints out to lay the hunt.
Under each empty frame Teo drew a little pencil sketch of what goes there: a wharf, a pump, two figures in the surf, a
helmet, a fish by a diver, a coin, seals, swallows, funnels, a cradle-boat, a mask head, a sleeping eye, and on the
thirteenth page, a boat with figures on it. These sketches are the game's hints, readable at any age, in any language,
with no words. Hollis never knew why the album was emptied. The player learns why at the end.

When eleven photographs are in (all but the last), the captain opens the album at the chart table himself (a short
wordless scene: he turns the pages, stops at the twelfth sketch, the sleeping eye), takes the pencil and draws the
fifth X on the chart, over the canyon floor: the place he could never bring himself to go back to. That X is how the
last chest is found (DESIGN.md: "the last chest cannot be found without the full album").

## The ending (wordless)

1. **The last dive.** The boat anchors over the canyon. The captain takes the pump himself. Jonah goes down the shot
   line in the deep suit, the winch cable beside him, to the canyon floor at about 100 m. And for the first and only
   time on a dive, Kit is with him: in the helmet, as a fish (whatever he is at the time; the amplifier's top step
   reaches 100 m), swimming at his brother's shoulder. So the picture forty years in the making is whole again: a
   diver below, a fish beside him, a man at the pump. The player is Jonah; Kit's fish follows him. In the dark the old
   one's sleeping head fills the view; its breath moves the silt. Under its chin lies Teo's deep suit, empty, sitting
   against a stone slab as if resting, its helmet's glass dark, its cut hose trailing off into the dark. The slab
   covers the last chest.
2. **What the player does.** Jonah hooks the winch to the slab; the boat lifts it. The old one stirs at the scrape;
   Kit's fish darts back to his brother's side. Jonah opens the last chest. In it, a sealed tin: the key with a ring
   tied to it, and photograph 12. The photograph is shown large (the question: a diver kneeling, and beside him the
   empty space where the fish should be). The camera pulls back to show Jonah kneeling at the chest with Kit's fish
   beside him: the space filled. Jonah sets the four-piece music box on the slab (he has carried it down from the chart
   table) and winds it with the key. It plays. The old one's breath slows and it settles deeper into sleep. Ghost fish,
   pale and lit, gather to listen. Jonah tugs the line; his grandfather hauls him up.
3. **On deck, at dusk.** Jonah, helmet off, hands the tin to his grandfather; Kit has taken the helmet off and comes
   out of the cabin. The captain looks at the photograph a long time (seen from behind and to the side: his shoulders,
   his hands, never a face doing the work). He unties the ring from the key and puts it on, beside the one he has worn
   on a cord round his neck for forty years (it shows in the open-shirt outfit if a builder wants to plant it early: a
   small gold ring on a cord in the chest hair). He goes into the cabin.
4. **The answer.** He comes out in the navy double-breasted coat with the red carnation (from the outfit sheet), the
   coat bought for a day that never came. He sits at the machine. Kit puts the helmet on his grandfather's head. Jonah
   takes the pump (the machine's crank, as on the title).
5. **The captain's swim (the only time the player is him).** The player controls a huge old grey giant sea bass for
   a short, slow, easy swim down the shot line through the evening water: no hunger, no hunters, nothing to eat, the
   panel's dials still. At the bottom of the line, in the sand where photograph 5 was taken, a diver of pale light in
   the old hard-hat stands waiting (the diver figure in the ghost whales' shader). The fish swims up to the helmet's
   front glass and touches it, as in photograph 5. The light-diver lifts one gloved hand to the fish's side, then fades
   into ghost fish that scatter upward. The player swims up. (If the user would rather no ghost, see the questions: the
   fish settles beside the empty helmet instead, and the ghost fish rise round it.)
6. **The final image.** On deck, the captain takes off the helmet. The three of them sit on the stern in the last
   light, the music box playing on the hatch cover between them. Out on the water a ghost whale's pale spout rises and
   falls. The game's camera takes a photograph: the frame freezes, tones to sepia, gets its deckled edge and drops into
   the album's thirteenth page, under Teo's sketch of a boat with figures on it, which it matches. The album closes.

After the ending the game goes on as before (fish runs, dives, fishing). The captain wears his carnation coat on the
title from then on, with both rings.

## How the rest touches the story

- **The captain's outfits.** Working clothes (the open navy coat over a striped shirt, yellow oilskins) are everyday.
  The cream cable-knit with the dark scarf was Teo's own jersey: he wears it on cold mornings. The bare-chested
  braces and bandana are hot work and show the tattoos. The striped bathing suit with the towel is the swim lesson of
  photograph 3, kept forty years: a joke he is in on (he wears it to sunbathe on deck and never goes in). The navy
  double-breasted coat with the white cap and red carnation is the wedding coat: unlocked only by the ending.
- **The tattoos** (the build script's flash: anchor, swallow, compass star, a ship on a ribbon, a heart, no letters).
  The swallow is the pair to Teo's (photograph 8): sailors' swallows mean coming home. The heart has no name in its
  ribbon, since a name could not be written then (no letters is already the rule of the flash; it becomes the
  meaning). The ship on the ribbon is the liner, the night Marina came to them. The anchor is the boat, home. The
  compass star is the way back.
- **The old one** sleeps over the last chest; it killed Teo by turning over, not by malice; the lullaby keeps it asleep.
- **The leviathan** is a harmless old giant scarred by a century of harpoons (side story F); it is what Kit sees
  first of the deep as a fish, and teaches that big is not the same as cruel.
- **The ghosts** (ghost whales, ghost fish) are the sea's memory: they gather where music is played. The light-diver
  at the end is the one ghost with a face the family knows.
- **The mermaids** have the mask faces of the Atlantis statue: the Atlanteans' children (side story C).
- **The liner** is where Marina came from: the family begins at the reef the user put at the chart's X.
- **The pirate ship** gave the gold for the rings and the comb, and was crewed by runaway children (side story B).
- **Atlantis** gave the lullaby and the green stone of the dancers.
- **The submarine** belonged to a naturalist whose drawings became the evolution tree (side story G); she and Teo
  knew of each other's work. One line of her sketchbook shows a diver with spectacles: a nod, not a plot.
- **The markets.** Monterey's is run by Teo's sister Lucia, now old (the boys call her Zia): Teo's people never stopped
  being Hollis's family. Santa Cruz's is the old angler's (side story D). Carmel's is Marina's.

## Tone

A love story between two men told with dignity and warmth: their life is shown as ordinary and good (work, jokes, a
picnic, a baby, a tattoo parlour), not as a secret or a punishment. The loss happened long ago and is shown by an
absence (an empty space beside a kneeling diver, a cut hose on a nail), never by a body or a scene of dying. The
story's present is about a grandfather, his grandsons and a question finally answered. There is room for the user's
humour throughout: the captain in a colander helmet, the swim lesson, the coin-biting, the sea lion stealing a
photograph, the river beast's hoard of hats. Suitable for a broad arcade audience: nothing in it is more than a
held hand, an arm round a shoulder, two figures turning on a music box, a kneeling man with a ring.

## What each one shows, and what the player understands

**1. The hand up.** The Monterey wharf, a bright morning. Teo, young, in a canvas diving dress with his helmet off
under one arm and his spectacles pushed up on his forehead, is climbing the wharf ladder out of the water. Young
Hollis, a deckhand in a jersey, kneels at the top and reaches down; their hands are clasped. *The one detail:* the
clasped hands, at the photograph's centre. *Understood:* two young men; one is a diver; the start of something.

**2. At the pump.** A dory off the Santa Cruz wharf. Hollis cranks a two-handled air pump; the hose runs over the
gunwale into the water, where a stream of bubbles rises. Hollis is not looking at the camera but over the side, at the
bubbles. *Detail:* one of his hands has left the crank to rest on the hose. *Understood:* he keeps the diver's air; he
watches over him. (The player has seen Jonah do the same on the title.)

**3. The swim lesson.** Knee-deep water off a beach, summer. Hollis in a striped bathing suit (the outfit sheet's),
standing stiff as a post, arms rigid; Teo in front of him, up to his waist, pulling him forward by both hands, leaning
back with laughter. *Detail:* Hollis's knees locked together. *Understood:* the captain cannot swim. A joke now; the
key to the ending later.

**4. The first helmet.** A net loft workshop: nets hanging, a bench of tools, wires, glass tubes, a car battery. Hollis
seated on a stool in a crude metal helmet like a colander with three tubes on top, arms folded, unimpressed; Teo
behind him, adjusting a wire, delighted. *Detail:* the helmet is plainly the ancestor of the one Kit wears. *Understood:*
the machine was made by the diver, for the man who could not swim. Funny.

**5. The fish and the diver.** Underwater, the sand by the pirate ship's stern, green light from above. Teo in a
hard-hat suit stands by the hull; a young giant sea bass, spotted orange, has its nose to the round front glass of his
helmet. *Detail:* Teo's gloved hand rests on the fish's side. *Understood:* the machine worked: the fish is Hollis. They
were together below.

**6. The doubloon.** The dory's deck, a bright afternoon, the pirate wreck's outcrop behind. Teo holds up a gold coin
between finger and thumb; Hollis has it between his teeth, biting it to test it. *Detail:* the coin. *Understood:* they
found the pirates' gold (and, with the comb in the same chest, that the gold was turned into something).

**7. The picnic on Seal Rock.** The flat rock among the stacks, golden hour, seen from behind them: the two sit side by
side on a blanket with a basket, Hollis's arm round Teo's shoulders, Teo's head on Hollis's shoulder. A sea lion is
pulling a fish out of their basket. *Detail:* the arm round the shoulders. *Understood:* they were a couple, plainly
and happily. (Found in the sea lion's hoard: the sea lions are still stealing from this family.)

**8. Two swallows.** A tattoo parlour by the wharf, flash sheets pinned on the wall. Hollis, bare-chested in braces,
sits with his forearm on a cushion while the tattooist (a figure seen from behind) works; Teo leans in beside him

*Understood:* a pledge (and the player can see the same swallow on the old captain's arm on deck).

**9. The morning after the liner.** Carmel beach at first light, fog. The liner's funnels stand out of the sea beyond
the point, tilted. The two young men sit wet on the sand wrapped in one blanket; Hollis holds a baby wrapped in a

**10. Three on the new boat.** The deck of a shrimp boat, freshly painted, at the Monterey commercial wharf: this

**11. Atlantis.** The ruins in the dark canyon, lit by one lamp. Teo, in the armoured suit with the lamp held high,

## Finding order and telling order

# PART THREE: STAGING BRIEFS (making the photographs in the game)

## The common recipe

Every photograph is a scene built from the game's own models, posed, and taken by the game's camera; then toned.

- **Tooling (proposal):** one `tools/photo_stage.gd` that loads a named scene (place, figures, outfits, props, a pose
  per bone group, the camera), renders at 1600x1200 (4:3, the shape of a cabinet card) or 1200x1600 for upright ones,
  and saves the raw picture; then one sepia pass (a post shader or a small image script), applied the same to all
  thirteen, the last one in real time at the ending.
- **The sepia pass:** desaturate, then map to a warm brown-cream ramp (shadows a dark umber, highlights a paper cream
  rather than white); lift the blacks a little; soft vignette; fine grain; two or three hairline scratches and a few
  dust specks (different per picture, chosen by seed); a slight blur at the corners (old lens). The edge: a deckled
  border (a cream paper margin with a torn-wave edge) for land pictures; for underwater pictures (5, 11, 12) a darker
  vignette with a round mask (the camera box's porthole). Damage per the table's state: water stain tide marks for
  loose ones, a crack line across the glass for framed ones, foxing spots for bottled.
- **Lens and look:** a long-ish lens (35 to 50 mm equivalent) at about chest height for people pictures: 1890s cameras
  on tripods, people posed and still, a level horizon. Underwater: a wide lens, low, looking slightly up toward the
  light.
- **Faces:** none of the briefs depends on a face. Each reads through the one detail named (hands, an arm, a space).
  With mask faces, turn heads a little toward each other; with simple stylised faces (the TP-style pass the user is
  weighing), give the young men small closed-mouth smiles and no more: old photographs were not grinning.
- **The two young men, as built:**
  - **Young Hollis:** the captain's build (the man with `heavy` and `body_gut` turned down to about half, so he is
    broad and thick but not yet stout), no beard (a clean jaw, perhaps a moustache-shadow), dark hair close-cropped,
    the captain's big nose. Clothes: the captain's gansey, oilskin trousers, the striped bathing suit, bare-chested in
    braces (with young chest hair and only the tattoos he had then: the swallow arrives in 8).
  - **Teo:** a new figure on the same man (Gloaming's man, no keys, a little taller and leaner), the mask head with
    a long moustache, round wire spectacles, dark hair; a flat cap on land. Clothes: shirt with rolled sleeves and a
    waistcoat, the diver's canvas dress (the diver's existing dress and helmet), the armoured suit and the deep suit
    from the diving-suit sheet when built for Jonah (shared models).
- **The baby and the toddler (Marina):** the baby is a wrapped bundle prop (a steamer rug folded round a small head
  shape: no figure needed). The toddler is the boy figure scaled to about 0.55 in a smock and a sun bonnet, posed
  seated on a hip.
- **The young sea bass:** the installed `sea_bass` creature as a juvenile (spotted orange), posed still.

# PART FOUR: OTHER STORIES

The captain's story is the spine; these are side stories the sea tells, each found through things on the floor and
readable without words. Each is a set of finds that go to the cabin's shelf (Things to find, below) in a row of their
own, with an empty silhouette for each missing piece, the same rule as the album. Completing a set gives one small
thing, never anything the spine needs. Gear steps refer to DESIGN.md (canvas 10 m, hard-hat 25 m, armoured 50 m, deep
100 m; lamp, crowbar, key ring, winch).

### Jonah's rod (how the fishing belongs to the story)

The user: "the older brother is the one who does the regular fishing". Jonah is the boat's angler, and the room
proposes his fishing carries three small threads, none a new system:

- **The money.** Fishing from the rail is the calm, steady earner (DESIGN.md); Jonah's catches go to the same three
  markets as Kit's fish runs. Each market's wall gets the first of each kind he lands as well (a trophy on a board).
- **The angler's eye.** What he lands at an anchorage shows that place's life before he dives it: a fish he catches
  over the pirate ship sometimes comes up with something in its gut or snagged on the hook (a doubloon, a lure from side
  story D, a pearl in an abalone fragment), a small find that marks the chart: the rod as a hint to where to walk.
- **The one that got away** (side story D): the giant lingcod is his to land.
- **At the ending** he sets the rod down on the rail to take the pump: the angler does not fish that evening.

### A. The liner's last night (the liner off Point Lobos, Carmel)

The liner struck the point in fog on a night of music, 1895. *Gear:* hard-hat for the upper decks, key ring for the
cabins, armoured for the engine room's low side. *Finds (6):* the ship's bell, its clapper tied with a bandsman's
scarf (rang no alarm: the band had been told to keep playing to keep the passengers calm); the bandmaster's cornet in
the saloon; a pocket watch stopped at ten past two; a silver serving tray with the line's crest (two waves and a
star: a crest, no words); the nursery's empty cradle (where photograph 9 lies); a lifeboat's bow plate with a painted
number, on the sand far from the wreck, toward Carmel. *Understood:* the ship went down while the band played; the
lifeboats got away, one plate drifted ashore at Carmel; one baby was missed in the nursery. *Completing gives:* the
ship's bell, hung on the shrimp boat; it rings when a catch is sold. *Touches the spine:* it is where Marina came from;
the lifebelt on Carmel market's wall is hers.

### B. The pirate ship's crew (the pirate ship and Seal Rock)

The "pirates" were runaway children. *Gear:* hard-hat, crowbar. *Finds (6):* a captain's tricorn far too small for a
man; a carved wooden toy whale; a cracked spyglass; a slate with a chalk drawing in a child's hand (a big man with a
whip, small figures running onto a ship, the ship sailing off: picture-writing); the figurehead's lost arm, in the
sand; on Seal Rock itself, small handprints and tally marks scratched into the stone above the waterline. *Understood:*
a crew of ship's boys and girls took their cruel master's ship and sailed it away; it struck the outcrop, and they all
got ashore on the seals' rock and lived there till they were found. *Completing gives:* the figurehead's arm put back
on the mermaid figurehead: she points down into the hold, where the crowbar chest is (a key to the chest the spine
needs; the chest can still be found without it). *Touches the spine:* their gold became the rings; children sailing
off on their own are Kit and Jonah's mirror.

### C. The lullaby of Atlantis (the ruins in the lower canyon)

Who built Atlantis, and what sleeps under it. *Gear:* armoured suit, lamp. *Finds (5):* four stone tablets of
picture-writing (masked people building a city on a mountain that breathes; the mountain opening an eye; the people
playing lyres and horns, the eye closing; the people walking into the sea and growing tails), and a stone lyre.
*Understood:* the city was built on the shoulder of the sleeping old one, and its people sang it to sleep for
generations; when they went into the sea (the mask-faced mermaids are their children), the giant rolled in its sleep
and the city slid into the canyon. *Completing gives:* the tune itself, which Teo pinned on the music box's cylinder
(so the box plays it once the set is done; before that the box is silent: a reason to finish C before the end,
not a lock on it). *Touches the spine:* the lullaby lets Jonah work under the old one's chin; the dancers on the lid are
carved from Atlantis's green stone.

### D. The one that got away (Santa Cruz wharf, the sand flats)

The fishermen's story, with the user's humour. An old angler has fished off the end of the Santa Cruz wharf every day
for forty years for one huge old lingcod, and runs the Santa Cruz market now with his son. *Gear:* canvas suit for the
wharf, hard-hat for the sand flats; the rod for the end. *Finds (6):* his lures, one per decade, lost in the sand, each
a different style (a wooden plug, a spoon, a feather jig...); his pipe; his hat; the snapped end of his best rod.
*Understood:* the same fish has broken him off for forty years. *The end:* the lingcod lives in the sand flats with
all six lures' hooks in its lip; Jonah, who finds the lures on his dives, can hook it from the rail with his rod once
all six are found (the fishing mode's one named fish, and the hardest fight in it). It is not sold: he brings it in, and the photograph-card for the shelf shows
the old man at the market holding it up, then letting it go off the wharf. *Completing gives:* the best tackle step
in the shop, cheaper. *Touches the spine:* he knew Hollis and Teo (he is a figure in the background of photograph 2,
fishing off the wharf, if a builder wants the nod).

### E. The beast of the slough (the mangrove river)

The furry crocodile-headed river beast (the user's own creature, made in that spirit, not a copy of the drawing) has
been stealing from fishermen for longer than anyone remembers. *Gear:* canvas suit; the lamp helps under the roots.
*Finds (6):* a heap of stolen soft things found round its den in the mangrove roots: a fisherman's sou'wester, a
child's knitted hat, a boot, a lunch pail, a bandana, a woollen sock. And photograph 8 in its cracked frame, used as a
lid. *Understood:* it is a mother lining a nest with warm things; three eggs lie in it. *The end:* when the eggs hatch
(after the set is complete, the next visit), the young follow the boat's wake up the river once and go back. *Completing
gives:* nothing to sell; the den's eggs become the shelf's set picture. *Touches the spine:* it took photograph 8, which
washed into the slough.

### F. The leviathan's harpoons (open ocean and the canyon's basin)

The leviathan is a harmless old giant (the user's "leviathan", built as one). *Gear:* hard-hat for the open-water
floor at the edge of the shelf, armoured for the basin. *Finds (5):* four harpoon heads of four different ages lying
along its path (each older one more rusted: a bone-and-iron one, a hand iron, a toggle iron, a bomb-lance), and a
whale's tooth with scrimshaw (a whaling ship and a great beast rising under it). Also on the floor, the whaler's
broken whaleboat. *Understood:* it has been hunted for a hundred years and has turned on its hunters only once;
it means no harm. *Completing gives:* the scrimshaw on the shelf; the leviathan lets the player's fish ride its
slipstream (if a builder wants a reward in play; otherwise nothing). *Touches the spine:* big is not the same as cruel,
the lesson the old one needs.

### G. The iron fish (the submarine, on the canyon wall past the shelf's edge)

A 1910s submarine, nose-down where it grounded. Its owner was a naturalist who drew everything she saw. *Gear:*
armoured suit, key ring for its hatch, lamp. *Finds (6):* her sketchbook in a watertight case (its plates are the
game's own creatures drawn in ink: the evolution tree's art); three specimen jars (a lanternfish, a firefly squid, a
baby anglerfish); her pocket compass; the escape buoy's cut line at the open top hatch. *Understood:* she got out, by
the buoy, and lived; the drawings are her life's work. *Completing gives:* the evolution tree's pages, drawn in her
hand (a reskin of the tree screen, not a new system). *Touches the spine:* one sketch shows a diver in spectacles
beside a spotted sea bass: she saw them once.

### H. Jonah's pearls (kelp, rocks, the outcrop)

The diver brother's own reason for being here. Jonah is saving pearls for someone on the Santa Cruz boardwalk (seen
only as a lit window and a figure who waves from the wharf when the boat passes; the room proposes leaving who it is
unshown). *Gear:* canvas suit for the kelp; hard-hat for the outcrop. *Finds (6):* abalone pearls (California's own:
baroque, green-blue), each in an abalone shell on the rocks; he prises them off himself and they go to his corner
of the shelf, not to market.
*Understood:* the big brother is courting. *Completing gives:* a sepia card for the shelf (Jonah at the boardwalk
rail, someone's hand in his) and his own work outfit changes to his best (a clean jersey) on the title.

# APPENDIX: HOW THE ROOM GOT HERE

## The three treatments

### Treatment 1: "The Proposal" (grounded; chosen, then blended)

A young deckhand who cannot swim and a diver who builds machines fall in love on the Monterey wharves in the 1890s.
The diver builds a helmet so his love can swim beside him as a fish; together they find the pirate ship, the liner
and Atlantis. The diver lays a treasure hunt across the bay, five chests that make a music box, with photographs of
their life laid along the way, as a proposal. He is lost laying the last chest under the sleeping old one. Forty years
on the captain, with his grandsons aboard, lets the younger one wear the helmet; the boys follow the hunt without
knowing what it is. The last chest holds the ring and a photograph of the diver kneeling with it, beside an empty
space. The captain puts on his best coat, wears the helmet, and goes below as a fish one last time; a photograph of
the three of them fills the album's last page.

### Treatment 2: "The Man from the Sea" (mythic)

The man the captain loved came out of the sea: one of the mask-faced people of Atlantis, who could walk on land only
for a season each year. Every photograph is of a summer they had. The treasure is the crown of Atlantis, which lets
its wearer stay ashore; the lover broke it into pieces and hid them so he could not be taken back. The boy, as fish
and diver, gathers the crown; at the end the captain must choose to give it back; he walks into the sea with his
lover, who has come up at the music of the box, and the boys see two mask-faced figures go down into the light.

### Treatment 3: "The Signal" (mad science)

The captain's love built the machine and, dying in a diving accident, put his mind into the sea through it: he is
the static at the machine's depth limit, a voice in the noise. The photographs are his memories, scattered by the
signal as it frayed. The boy gathers them to rebuild his mind; the gear ladder is the amplifier reaching him. At the
end the signal is clear: the lover's mind is in an old whale, and the captain puts on the helmet and swims with him.

## The room's criticism

**Treatment 1.**
- *Story editor:* moves well: every chest is a step and the last one turns everything round (the photographs were
  laid, not lost). The ending lands because it is an answer and not only a loss: the old man does the one thing he
  could never do. Risk: a dead lover is a familiar trope for gay stories; the room answers it by setting the death
  forty years back, never showing it, and making the present about family, an answer and joy.
- *Game designer:* every beat sits on a place DESIGN.md already has, a gear step it already sells and a chest it
  already plans; the photographs give each dive a reason; the album's pencil sketches are a wordless quest log; the
  ending reuses the fish run with its systems off. Tellable with no dialogue: yes, through objects.
- *The user's taste:* closest to the user's own painting *Finding* (a diver on the reef before a wreck: "Well, I've
  never really found what I was looking for anyways...") and *Red Rabbit* ("I was just a boy when I found you"): a
  search whose treasure is a person. Warm and funny, as their Eddie and Owen pictures are.

**Treatment 2.**
- *Story editor:* a strong fairy-tale shape, but the ending is the captain leaving his grandsons: sad for a family
  game, and it reads as death.
- *Game designer:* the crown is a good collectible; a lover who comes and goes by season has nowhere to stand in a
  game set in one summer; the walk into the sea needs a new scene with no play in it.
- *The user's taste:* uses the mask faces and Atlantis well (the Atlantis concept's statue and the mermaid concept
  both have the figures' mask face), and the liminal Other World the user named for Warren's lore. Kept: the
  Atlanteans and mermaids as side story C.

**Treatment 3.**
- *Story editor:* clever, but explains too much and too strangely; the photographs scattered by a signal makes no
  physical sense, and the ending depends on a rule (minds in whales) the player has to be told.
- *Game designer:* the static at the depth limit and the amplifier as the way to a voice is the neatest fit of gear to
  story of the three; but a voice needs words, and the game has almost none.
- *The user's taste:* the mad-science machine as an act of love is very theirs (the old machines, The Simulatrix's lab,
  the Frankenstein panel). Kept: the machine built as a gift; dropped: the trapped mind.

**Choice:** Treatment 1, with the machine as a love gift from 3, and the Atlanteans and mermaids of 2 as a side story
and the source of the lullaby. Then reshaped for the user's later words: the captain is the boys' grandfather (so the
liner gives the couple their daughter), and the other stories and finds were added.
